This paper discusses the complexity of the religious traditions in Quanzhou (Fujian, China), the largest international trade port under Mongol rule. The contribution of presumed Persian Muslim Pu Shougeng 蒲壽庚to the reconstruction of a Taoist-Buddhist shrine was taken as the case study. The external conditions surrounding his composite religious act (beyond private beliefs) were also observed in terms of individual goals, backgrounds, and social networks. For this purpose, the author presents the Chinese stone inscription from Quanzhou (in Fujian, China) titled “Zhong jian Qingyuan Chunyang dong ji 重建清源純陽洞記 (Record of Reconstruction of the Chunyang Cave in Qingyuan Mountain),” dated to the fourth year of Hou-Zhiyuan 後至元 (1338) during the Yuan period.

Omidsalar ([1389] 2010–2011, [1393] 2014–2015) has forcefully pushed back against scholarly assumptions of Rabīʿ’s total hostility to the Shāh nāma and its contents. While I share Omidsalar’s opposition to a simplistic reading of the ʿAlī nāma as evidence for a generalised hostility between Shīʿī Muslims and the stuff of Iranian ‘national’ epic ([1389] 2010–2011, 52–54), I will argue below that the antipathy that Rabīʿ’s poem projects towards Ferdowsi is real and substantial, expressing not a universal sentiment but the particular mimetic concerns of Rabīʿ himself. Specific points of divergence with Omidsalar’s arguments in this regard will be addressed below as and when they appear.

On the other hand, social-scientific research is increasingly required to ensure gender-neutral access (see Raabe 2017). Faced with this conflict of accessibility and restrictions, the authors have chosen to depict the objects discussed here through simplified line drawings that are not equivalent to original photographs.↩︎
